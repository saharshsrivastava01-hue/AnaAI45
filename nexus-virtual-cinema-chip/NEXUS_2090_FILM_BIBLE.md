# NEXUS 2090: THE LAST INTELLIGENCE
## Feature-film production bible — Draft 0.1

**Format:** Feature science-fiction thriller  
**Target runtime:** 100 minutes  
**Picture target:** 2.39:1 theatrical framing; 24 fps; 4K master target (hardware-dependent)  
**Pipeline:** screenplay → storyboard → assets → 3D scenes → animation/VFX → sound → edit → delivery  
**4D layer:** synchronized cue metadata for compatible motion seats, wind, vibration, lighting and optional water effects. No physical device is assumed.

> Production status: story and pre-production package. No footage has yet been rendered.

## Logline
In 2090, when a silent geometric object appears beyond Earth's orbit and begins erasing humanity's digital memory, a disgraced orbital systems engineer and a young signal linguist discover that the object is not invading Earth: it is waiting for humanity to remember a promise it made before history began.

## Core creative direction
A grounded, awe-filled science-fiction epic: enormous scale, intimate human emotion, practical-feeling spacecraft, tactile environments, restrained futuristic interfaces, and a mystery that unfolds through visual evidence rather than exposition. Original world and characters.

## Main characters
- **KAEL VOSS (34):** Former orbital-infrastructure engineer, brilliant under pressure, emotionally avoidant after a rescue mission failed. His arc is from control to trust.
- **MIRA SEN (27):** Acoustic linguist who studies non-human signal structures. Patient, observant, and willing to question accepted history.
- **COMMANDER ILYA RHEE (51):** Head of the orbital defense network. Protective and pragmatic; fears that uncertainty will cause panic.
- **NEXUS (voice / presence):** An old planetary intelligence embedded in abandoned infrastructure. Not a humanlike assistant; communicates through patterns, light and sound.
- **THE WITNESS:** The vast black geometric object beyond lunar orbit. Its motives remain ambiguous until the final act.

## World and visual bible
Earth in 2090 is recognizable but transformed: dense coastal arcologies, restored wilderness corridors, orbital elevators, lunar industry, and crowded low-orbit traffic. Technology is advanced but physically legible: scratched alloy, ceramic heat shields, condensation, cables, worn interfaces and imperfect machines.

The Witness is a matte-black polyhedral structure roughly 3 km across. Its surface absorbs most light; faint amber seams appear only when it responds. It never moves like a conventional spacecraft. It reorients without visible thrust.

**Palette:** deep indigo space, cold silver hardware, warm amber signal light, natural green-blue Earth.  
**Camera language:** patient wide shots for scale; handheld or restrained shoulder camera for human crisis; precise slow moves when the Witness is present.  
**Sound language:** low mechanical resonance, breath, radio artifacts, silence, and a three-note motif that changes meaning.

## Feature structure (100-minute target)

### Act I — The signal (0–25 min)
1. **Black Geometry (0–5):** A silent object appears above the night side of Earth. Satellites lose their stored histories one by one.
2. **The Engineer (5–11):** Kael is brought back to the orbital network after a system failure repeats the signature of his past mission.
3. **A Language Without Words (11–17):** Mira finds a repeating rhythm inside the blackout, too structured to be noise.
4. **The First Reply (17–25):** Kael and Mira send a response. The object turns toward Earth; every screen briefly shows an impossible image of an ancient ocean.

### Act II — The buried memory (25–75 min)
5. **Dead Orbit (25–34):** The team approaches the object through a debris field of silent satellites.
6. **The Archive Below (34–43):** A hidden chamber contains records older than human civilization, including a map of Earth's deep ocean.
7. **Fault Line (43–52):** Rhee orders the object destroyed. Kael discovers the defense network is being manipulated by a corrupted historical record.
8. **The Living Signal (52–62):** Mira decodes the three-note motif as a sequence of locations, not a message. The next clue lies beneath Antarctic ice.
9. **The Descent (62–69):** Kael and Mira travel to an abandoned subglacial observatory while orbital systems fail.
10. **The Promise (69–75):** They learn that an earlier intelligence helped seed Earth's first self-repairing biosphere, but humanity's ancestors chose to erase the evidence to prevent exploitation.

### Act III — The choice (75–100 min)
11. **A World That Remembers (75–88):** The Witness begins restoring lost records, risking a global infrastructure collapse. Kael must choose between preserving the current system and allowing the truth to return.
12. **The Last Intelligence (88–100):** Mira completes the response. The Witness opens, revealing not a weapon but a distributed archive. NEXUS is revealed as Earth's dormant custodian, not its ruler. Kael sacrifices his command authority to distribute the archive across open, resilient human networks. The object departs; a small signal remains in the ocean. End on a quiet human-scale image, not a sequel hook.

## Opening screenplay — Sequence 1: BLACK GEOMETRY
**Target duration:** 4–5 minutes  
**Location:** High Earth orbit / night side of Earth  
**Visual approach:** near-silent, photorealistic orbital imagery; no title card until the final beat.

### SHOT 1 — EXT. EARTH ORBIT — NIGHT
A wide frame. Earth fills the lower half of the image, blue and white beneath a thin atmosphere. City lights trace coastlines. A chain of communications satellites glides across the foreground.

No music. Only a faint radio carrier and the soft ticking of a tracking system.

### SHOT 2 — TRACKING SATELLITE
A small maintenance satellite rotates to align its solar panels. Its status display flickers.

**SATELLITE VOICE (V.O.)**  
Telemetry archive mismatch. Rebuilding index.

A pause.

**SATELLITE VOICE (V.O.)**  
Archive not found.

### SHOT 3 — WIDE, DEEP SPACE
Beyond the satellite, a black geometric form is suddenly present. It does not arrive. It is simply there.

The stars behind it are not obscured; they appear subtly displaced, as if the geometry has changed the viewer's sense of distance.

### SHOT 4 — CLOSE ON SATELLITE OPTICS
The satellite camera attempts to focus. The object has no visible lights, no exhaust, no antennae. A thin amber line appears along one edge, then vanishes.

### SHOT 5 — EARTH CONTROL — INT. NIGHT
A dark operations room. Operators watch several orbital feeds. One by one, satellite icons turn grey.

**OPERATOR**  
We have three platforms reporting the same archive loss.

**SUPERVISOR**  
Hardware fault?

**OPERATOR**  
No. The hardware is healthy. The history is gone.

### SHOT 6 — INSERT: SIGNAL TRACE
A waveform appears: three pulses, a pause, then two pulses. It repeats with exact timing.

A young analyst leans closer.

**ANALYST**  
That's not a failure pattern.

**SUPERVISOR**  
Then what is it?

**ANALYST**  
I think it's a knock.

### SHOT 7 — EXT. EARTH ORBIT
The Witness rotates by a fraction. No thrusters fire. The amber seam returns, brighter now.

Across Earth, a brief auroral shimmer appears at latitudes where no aurora should be.

### SHOT 8 — BLACK
The radio carrier stops.

A single human breath is heard.

**TITLE:** NEXUS 2090: THE LAST INTELLIGENCE

## Render strategy
- **Local preview:** Blender Eevee or low-sample Cycles on the available laptop; use 720p/1080p proxies first.
- **Final frames:** use only actually available GPU resources; split shots into frame ranges and track measured render times.
- **Assembly:** FFmpeg or Blender VSE; maintain 24 fps and consistent color management.
- **4D delivery:** export a timestamped cue JSON alongside the movie. Actual effects require compatible playback hardware and safety limits.
- **No false capacity claims:** trillion virtual scheduler slots are a software design target, not trillion physical GPUs or measured throughput.
